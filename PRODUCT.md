# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Epistable serves food manufacturers, processors, Indian exporters, supplier teams, quality/compliance teams, and international food buyers. Buyers use the portal to discover suppliers and available products, compare evidence, review certifications and test reports, request quotes, and judge whether a supplier and lot are suitable for their market and specification.

## Product Purpose

Epistable is a verified trade workspace for food buyers and exporters. It helps suppliers turn product, quality, compliance, traceability, and shipment records into buyer-ready evidence: product specifications, lot/batch data, COAs, lab reports, certifications, traceability records, quotes, audit records, and export-readiness documents.

Epistable should help a buyer answer five questions quickly:
1. Can I trust this supplier?
2. Does this product or lot meet my specification?
3. Are the relevant certifications and test reports current?
4. Can this supplier ship to my destination market?
5. What evidence supports the supplier's claims?

Epistable organizes and verifies evidence where possible but must not imply that Epistable itself is an accredited certification body or laboratory.

## Core Commercial Journey

Supplier onboarding -> product catalog -> lot/batch availability -> quality & compliance evidence -> buyer discovery -> RFQ -> quote -> buyer decision -> shipment-readiness documents.

## Initial Food Categories

Start with export-oriented categories where documentation, testing, and traceability materially affect buyer decisions:
- Spices and seasonings
- Fresh produce
- Processed fruit and fruit ingredients
- Nuts and dry fruits
- Food ingredients

The architecture should remain category-extensible.

## Buyer Evidence Layer

A buyer-facing product/lot page should prioritize:
- Supplier and facility identity
- Product name and specification
- Lot/batch number
- Origin
- Available quantity and MOQ
- Production/harvest date and shelf life where applicable
- Packaging configuration
- Storage conditions
- COA / specification sheet
- Microbiological, chemical, pesticide/residue, allergen, or other relevant lab reports
- Facility certifications such as BRCGS, FSSC 22000, ISO 22000, HACCP, Organic, Halal, Kosher, or buyer-specific schemes where applicable
- Traceability evidence
- Destination-market readiness
- Evidence status and validity dates
- RFQ / Request quote action

## Brand Personality

Calm, credible, international, procurement-grade. The interface should feel like a serious food sourcing, quality, and compliance workspace, not a generic marketplace or playful startup dashboard.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic marketplace clutter, pay-to-win supplier badges that look like verification, loud AI gradients, decorative emojis, cluttered KPI walls, and language that implies "Epistable certified". Avoid hiding products and evidence behind a dashboard. Do not confuse paid visibility with evidence quality.

## Design Principles

1. Put the working object first: products, lots, evidence, RFQs, quotes, and documents should be immediately visible.
2. Reduce training burden: each role sees only the workflows they need.
3. Trust comes from evidence: show source, status, lab/certification details, validity, and traceability.
4. Keep the UI familiar: procurement and export users should recognize search, filters, product cards, evidence tables, and RFQ actions instantly.
5. Preserve neutrality: Epistable organizes evidence and readiness; accredited laboratories and certification bodies issue formal test reports and certifications.
6. Make market readiness explicit: buyers should understand whether evidence is relevant to their destination market and specification.
7. Separate commercial ranking from trust: sponsored visibility must never alter evidence or verification status.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use legible type, clear contrast, standard controls, keyboard-accessible actions, and responsive layouts that work on mobile for factory/export teams and buyers.
