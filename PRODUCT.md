# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Epistable serves processed food manufacturers, processors, Indian exporters, supplier teams, quality/compliance teams, and international food buyers. Buyers use the portal to discover suppliers and products, compare evidence, review certifications and test reports, request quotes, and judge whether a supplier, product, and lot are suitable for their market and specification.

## Product Purpose

Epistable is a verified trade workspace for processed food buyers and exporters. It helps suppliers turn product, quality, compliance, traceability, and shipment records into buyer-ready evidence: product specifications, lot/batch data, COAs, lab reports, certifications, allergen declarations, traceability records, quotes, audit records, and export-readiness documents.

Epistable should help a buyer answer five questions quickly:
1. Can I trust this supplier and facility?
2. Does this product or lot meet my specification?
3. Are the relevant certifications and test reports current?
4. Can this supplier ship to my destination market?
5. What evidence supports the supplier's claims?

Epistable organizes and verifies evidence where possible but must not imply that Epistable itself is an accredited certification body or laboratory.

## Core Commercial Journey

Supplier onboarding -> factory capability -> product/SKU catalog -> lot/batch availability -> quality & compliance evidence -> buyer discovery -> RFQ -> sample/spec review -> quote -> buyer decision -> shipment-readiness documents.

## Initial Processed Food Categories

Start with export-oriented processed food categories where repeat SKUs, documentation, testing, and recurring buyer relationships make the workflow sticky:
- Spices & seasonings
- Sauces, condiments & chutneys
- Processed fruit, pulps, purees & concentrates
- Ready-to-eat / ready-to-cook foods
- Frozen processed foods
- Bakery, confectionery & snack products
- Oils, fats & processed ingredients
- Dehydrated foods
- Beverage products & concentrates
- Food ingredients & premixes

Fresh produce and nutraceuticals are explicitly out of scope for the launch version. Organic, Halal, Kosher, Vegan, Non-GMO, Fairtrade, sustainability and responsible-sourcing programs are cross-category attributes/evidence overlays, not separate product bases.

## Buyer Evidence Layer

A buyer-facing product/lot page should prioritize:
- Supplier and facility identity
- Product name and SKU/specification
- Ingredients and formulation summary where appropriate
- Allergen declaration
- Lot/batch number
- Country/place of origin
- Available quantity and MOQ
- Production date, expiry/best-before, and shelf life
- Packaging configuration and private-label capability
- Storage conditions
- COA / specification sheet
- Microbiological, chemical, pesticide/residue, heavy-metal, contaminant, or other relevant lab reports
- Facility certifications such as BRCGS, FSSC 22000, ISO 22000, HACCP and applicable market/customer schemes
- Product claims and overlay evidence such as Organic, Halal, Kosher, Vegan, Non-GMO, Fairtrade or sustainability where applicable
- Traceability evidence
- Destination-market readiness
- Evidence status and validity dates
- RFQ / Request quote action

## Brand Personality

Calm, credible, international, procurement-grade. The interface should feel like a serious processed-food sourcing, quality, and compliance workspace, not a generic marketplace or playful startup dashboard.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic marketplace clutter, pay-to-win supplier badges that look like verification, loud AI gradients, decorative emojis, cluttered KPI walls, and language that implies "Epistable certified". Avoid hiding products and evidence behind a dashboard. Do not confuse paid visibility with evidence quality.

## Design Principles

1. Put the working object first: products, lots, evidence, RFQs, quotes, samples/specifications, and documents should be immediately visible.
2. Reduce training burden: each role sees only the workflows they need.
3. Trust comes from evidence: show source, status, lab/certification details, validity, and traceability.
4. Keep the UI familiar: procurement and export users should recognize search, filters, product cards, evidence tables, and RFQ actions instantly.
5. Preserve neutrality: Epistable organizes evidence and readiness; accredited laboratories and certification bodies issue formal test reports and certifications.
6. Make market readiness explicit: buyers should understand whether evidence is relevant to their destination market and specification.
7. Separate commercial ranking from trust: sponsored visibility must never alter evidence or verification status.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use legible type, clear contrast, standard controls, keyboard-accessible actions, and responsive layouts that work on mobile for factory/export teams and buyers.
